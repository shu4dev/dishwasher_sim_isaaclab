# Leaning bowl pairs: can a clear release reach a jointly settled goal one dish at a time? (plus the New+J explorer curve)
Slug: 2026-10-14-newj-curve-and-leaning-pairs

## Question
Can the benchmark's one-dish-at-a-time move reach a jointly settled goal that holds a mutually leaning bowl pair (5 of the 11 gated restricted loads, read as cycles by the ordered-pair test) if the dish is released from the lowest collision-free hover and a placed dish counts as disturbed only when it ends outside its own goal tolerance, and does a graph whose need edges come from the removal re-settles order the loads with fewer learned constraints and more edges that survive a perturbed release?

## What's known (max 5)
- This repo, run note `results/2026-10-14-pairwise-dependency-graphs.md` (page Version 19): of 17 restricted loads 11 pass the joint gate, 6 build (5 at the first build), 5 are cycles, every cycle a lower-rack bowl pair in one zone (lower_frontright or lower_rear), touching at the jointly settled pose. The first bowl relaxes 1.3-34.5 mm when its partner is absent; the partner, released 3 mm above its goal, lands 7.5-11.2 mm deep inside the relaxed bowl and the settle kicks the pair (partner moved 7.7-36.6 mm / 11.6-56.8 deg; the dropped bowl ends 10-42 mm / 9-37 deg off). One bowl stands alone within tolerance in 3 of 5 loads (r00_00031 bowl_06, r05_00026 bowl_06, r13_00038 bowl_05; relax 1.3-16.1 mm), neither bowl in r02_00048 and r02_00021. The own-load re-settle failed on the same pairs (contact 8.2-9.0 mm). PhysX is bit-for-bit deterministic; under a 3 mm / 3 deg release perturbation 15 of 21 edges reproduced, every bowl cycle held, both plate cycles flipped. Why it matters: the cycles are physical mutual support, and every kick starts from a release inside a relaxed neighbour; the removal re-settles already show which dish falls without which (drift 7-84 mm), but only the largest drift is recorded.
- Ren and Qureshi, IROS 2024 (arXiv 2305.17175): edge B -> A when A's goal footprint meets B's placing tunnel from the robot's point; the graph comes from the goal alone, acyclicity is assumed (cycles are never mentioned; a straight tunnel from one point orders every edge front to back), no physics. Graph-ordered stages vs a random order, 60 s budget: 100.00% vs 57.50% success at 7-8 objects (160 instances), 100.00% vs 88.75% at 4-6. Why it matters: the ordered stages are worth keeping for the acyclic part; the paper has no fix for a cycle.
- Han, Stiffler, Krontiris, Bekris, Yu, IJRR 2018 (arXiv 1711.07369) and Gao, Yu, IROS 2022 (arXiv 2207.08078): an edge is a goal footprint overlapping another start footprint; cycles are broken by buffers, and the minimum number of extra grasps equals the minimum feedback vertex set (NP-hard; the ILP solves 30+ objects in seconds); Gao and Yu resolve a two-object cycle by moving both objects at once with two arms. No dependency lives inside the goal arrangement. Why it matters: a buffer cannot break a goal-internal cycle (whichever bowl goes first fails); the only analogue is a simultaneous two-object placement.
- Wilson and Latombe, AI 71 (1994), and ASAP, Tian et al., ICRA 2024 (arXiv 2309.16909): parts that cannot be added one at a time are joined in one nonlinear instruction with every moved set grasped or fixtured (W-L); ASAP never inserts two parts at once, holds a part that would fall (budget M) or marks the step infeasible, stability by a penalty-contact simulation on the remaining parts. ASAP on 240 assemblies: 51-55% success with 2 holds and 50 evaluations, 81-82% with 4 holds at 400; half of its failures have no feasible sequence ("loosely assembled parts"). Why it matters: a leaning pair placed one at a time with no hold is exactly the step these planners call infeasible; the known outs are a hold, a joint move, or rejection.
- Furrer et al., ICRA 2017 (stone stacking) and Paxton et al., CoRL 2021 (arXiv 2108.12062): both release from clearance (Furrer offsets each candidate off the stack along the support normal and lets the simulation find contact, then re-plans from the measured pose; Paxton releases 2 cm above the goal) and judge success on the settled pose (CoM inside the support polygon, 3 or more contacts, kinetic energy under 20 J; Paxton: centre moves under 5 cm). Furrer: 2 of 11 real runs stacked all 4 stones, 6 of 11 three; Paxton: 84 of 100 simulated placements succeeded. Neither measures release sensitivity at the millimetre scale. Why it matters: a clear release judged on the settled pose is standard practice; the sensitivity has to be measured here.

## Gap
No rearrangement or assembly paper reaches a mutual lean one object at a time without a hold, and nobody has tested whether a gravity settle can do it when the release starts clear of the relaxed partner and the partner is allowed to move back into its goal; our only evidence is negative and comes from releases that started 7-11 mm inside the neighbour. Nor has anyone built the need edges from what falls when a dish is removed, which the test already simulates and discards.

## Candidate ideas (max 3)
1. Lowest clear hover plus a goal-judged disturbance, one dish per move, and need edges from the removal re-settles: raise the release in 1 mm steps above the goal until the collision pieces are at least 1 mm from every placed dish and the appliance, measured against the context's re-settled poses (cap 60 mm; beyond it "no clear release", not landed, block evidence; no lateral search); count a placed dish as disturbed only if it ends outside its own at-goal tolerance (bowls 15 mm lateral / 20 mm height / 15 deg; plates 18 / 20 / 16); need(A, X) whenever X leaves its tolerance in the re-settle with A parked (per-dish drift, no drop), block and knock from the drops, a drop whose context holds a fallen dish rerun with that dish parked too; margins stored per drop, drops within 20% of a threshold rerun under 3 perturbed releases (majority); the build's learned constraints join the graph. Might work because the kick comes from penetration at release, the relax is measured exactly by the re-settle, and in 3 of 5 loads one bowl stands alone with a relax of 1.3-16.1 mm, near or inside the tolerance. Might fail because a 20-60 mm fall can bounce or tip the bowl, the second bowl may slide off the lean instead of into it, and a relax over 15 mm fails the goal-judged rule anyway (r02_00048 and r02_00021: 18-35 mm, no bowl stands alone). Cheapest test: the full new test on all 11 gated loads (24 removal re-settles, 24 last drops, 2 drops per candidate pair within 20 mm, the invalid-context and marginal reruns, then the build; a mutual-support load gets a diagnostic build with the pair placed jointly), plus the perturbed rerun on the 4 first loads: 15 Kit sessions, about 2 h of wall time, paired against Version 18 on extra constraints (v18: 0 on five loads, 4 on r07_00023) and edges reproduced under perturbation (v18: 15 of 21).
2. Place-together node: contract each two-way pair (strongly connected component) into one node; the build teleports both dishes at goal + 3 mm and settles once; the benchmark gets a two-dish move (Wilson-Latombe's nonlinear instruction, Gao-Yu's simultaneous move). Might work because the joint settle is what produced the goal. Might fail because the move count, the counter cap, the sequencer and the compat optimum assume one dish per move, and a pair move needs both dishes on the counter at once. Cheapest test: teleport each cyclic pair together into its full context, one settle per load (5 settles); idea 1's diagnostic build does this as a side result.
3. Reject at generation: only goals the sequential build reproduces (the HOTEC bench's sequencing certificate) become goals; cyclic loads are re-rolled. Might work because 6 of 11 gated loads already build and the best certified built S under New+J (0.1612, r11_00034) is within 0.006 of the best cyclic settled S (0.1674, r02_00048). Might fail because it avoids the question and drops 5 of the 11 best loads. Cheapest test: none, the data already names the certified loads.

## Picked
1, because it keeps one dish per move, the benchmark's move set and the optimum unchanged, and the literature's other outs (a hold, a joint move) are not available to a benchmark without an arm; 2 is the fallback the literature supports for the pairs whose first bowl relaxes beyond its tolerance, and it is what "place together or re-roll" means on the page; 3 stays the bench's existing rule.

## Decided (interviews, 2026-10-14)
- Failure mode: the two-way pairs, the release sensitivity and the lean drift are one problem: the goal is a joint equilibrium and the test reads single drops.
- Fix family: the move's release (lowest clear hover, 1 mm clearance, the farm's rule) plus the disturbance judged against the goal, applied alike in the pair test, the sequential build and the Frigidaire episodes (a dish not at its goal keeps the counted nudge); the Bosch benchmark stays untouched. Rejected: a two-dish move (changes the benchmark), goal filtering (avoids the question), a test-only change (the graph would promise orders the benchmark refuses), a lateral release search, any-positive or 2 mm clearance.
- Graph purpose: planner constraints and feasibility, soundness first; a false edge is the worse error, missing edges are learned by the build. Rejected: a difficulty statistic (robustness first), a certificate only.
- Edge evidence: need from the removal re-settles (per-dish drift beyond tolerance), block and knock from drops under the new move; a drop into an invalid context (a fallen dish) is rerun with that dish parked too. Rejected: drops only; a Kit-free support-and-vertical-access graph with Isaac certifying only; skipping or merely flagging invalid-context drops.
- Two-way pairs: two-way need = mutual support, "place together or re-roll"; a two-way pair of knock or block only is retested and, if it stays, a cycle. Rejected: every two-way pair a cycle as now; contracting the pair into one node (needs the rejected move).
- Robustness: margins recorded; drops within 20% of a threshold rerun under 3 perturbed releases, majority verdict. Rejected: 3 releases for every drop (3x cost), single run with margins only.
- Learned constraints join the published graph (reason "learned"); candidate pairs stay at 20 mm between collision pieces. Rejected: learned constraints outside the graph; testing partial contexts directly; 15 cm or every pair.
- Better means: extra constraints per load and edges reproduced under the 3 mm / 3 deg release, paired against Version 18 on the 11 gated loads, plus how many of the 5 cycles are true mutual support. This supersedes the earlier "five cyclic loads first" scope. Rejected: the cyclic loads only; builds needed.
- Proving run: 11 sessions plus the 4 perturbed sessions, about 2 h of wall time, no pilot; a mutual-support load gets a diagnostic build with the pair placed jointly (not a benchmark move, captioned as such). Rejected: a two-load pilot; no perturbed rerun; no build; a kinematic hold of the first bowl.
- Page: new graphs only, one edge style, plus one table comparing v18 and the new graphs per load (edges, extra constraints, cycles, reproduced share); the v18 graphs stay in Version 19 and the run note. Rejected: both graphs per load; no comparison on the page.
- File name: today's date, 2026-10-14.
- Clearance of the clear hover (decided 2026-10-14 18:55 UTC, after the runner's Kit-free measurement): the farm's actual rule for both the placed dishes and the appliance, the lowest hover whose pose passes the bench checker's 1 mm penetration allowance (not a 1 mm gap). A 1 mm gap from the appliance is degenerate (5-8 dishes per load rest on tines whose contact runs along the lift, FCL distance 0 up to 60 mm) and a 1 mm gap from the dishes alone gives 0-3 dishes per load no clear release even with every dish at its goal (touching pairs would become two-way block pairs, false cycles). Under the allowance every goal pose passes at hover 0 and the hover rises only where a relaxed neighbour intrudes more than 1 mm, the v18 kick case. Rejected: a 1 mm gap from the dishes with the allowance for the appliance; the literal gap.
- Perturbed releases (runner's call, 2026-10-14 19:04 UTC, endorsed): the 3 mm / 3 deg perturbation is applied to the release pose the clear-hover search finds at the unperturbed target, as v18's mechanism did to target + 3 mm, so the reruns measure release noise; perturbing the goal first and then searching the hover drove a tine-nestled bowl's hover to 48 mm (a fall, not noise). Rejected: perturb the goal, then search.

## Explorer curve and relative score (item 1, decided in the same interviews)
- The explorer keeps one rule, New+J, one curve per entity (dot, plate, bowl, cup; dot | object level). Original, D, D+G, D+J, New and New+G leave the explorer and its legend; the grid file and the appendix keep them.
- Show = score | relative to 0 deg (replacing "difference from the original", which needs Original). Relative divides by the same dish at 0 deg (axis straight down, the grid's definition of facing the arm, not a maximum) at the same height, distance and theta: 1 at every 0 deg pose, 0 at 180 deg; drawn only where the 0 deg value is at least 0.05 (below it a gap and a note "reference too small": 30-35 cm out, where even the facing dish barely sees the disc); above 1 in a few percent of cells (cup at most 1.14 at dot level), the axis grows. The page states that the cup's best tilt under Original is 25 deg (0.432 against 0.420 at 0 deg). Rejected: the max over tilt (the cup's peak is not at 0 deg in 16% of cells), one constant per dish, the dish turned toward the disc centre (needs toward-centre tilts, a grid extension of hours), a gap only at exactly 0, a hatched band.
- What the relative view will show (grid, N = 262,144): over the disc centre the three dishes' relative New+J curves coincide (14 cm up, theta 30: plate / bowl / cup keep 0.867 / 0.865 / 0.863 at 30 deg, 0.709 / 0.707 / 0.703 at 45, 0.504 / 0.501 / 0.499 at 60, 0.125 / 0.124 / 0.127 on edge; spread at most 0.006 over all tilts), so New+J's tilt response there is a property of the jets, not of the dish, and the dish only sets the ceiling (0.814 / 0.504 / 0.183). Near the disc edge they separate (median spread over the 493 cells with all three references at or above 0.05 is 0.126, up to 0.82 at 8 cm up, 25 cm out, theta 10). Under Original they never coincide (on edge the plate keeps 81%, the bowl 42%, the cup 20%; median spread 0.57): this is the page's main argument in relative terms.
- Comparison on the page: one sentence per dish in the per-dot captions with the share kept on edge under Original and under New+J. The explorer stays New+J only. Rejected: Original as a grey reference curve; nothing new.
- Relative S, a column next round (scored, not adopted): a dish's New+J over its own reference, the same engine run on that dish alone, axis straight down, at its own centre under its own arm, N = 262,144 (about 7,300 dish runs for the 17 restricted loads in three states and the 252 random loads, under an hour, Kit-free); the reference floored at 0.05 (relative = New+J / max(reference, 0.05), so a hopeless spot stays low and no dish is excluded); area-weighted like S; shown for every scored load (a "relative" row in the random-loads S table, a panel in the spread figure, columns in the dependency table), with the rank correlation to absolute S stated once. Estimate from the lower-arm grid by nearest cell: Spearman 0.93 over the 252 random loads, 1 of the top 5 in common, 602 of 6,048 dishes with a reference under 0.05. Rejected: nearest grid cell (the middle arm's disc is 4 cm smaller), one constant per kind, excluding or capping the tiny-reference dishes, a plain mean, random loads only, a separate section.
