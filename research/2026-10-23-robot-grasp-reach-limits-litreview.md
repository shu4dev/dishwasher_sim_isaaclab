# Literature review: grasp and reach limits of a UR5e + Robotiq 2F-85 loading mouth-up dishware into dishwasher racks
Date: 2026-10-23 | Mode: ARS lit-review (bibliography, citation verification, synthesis) | Question: for a UR5e with a Robotiq 2F-85 loading plates, bowls and cups lying mouth-up on a counter into dishwasher racks, what does the literature establish about (1) two-finger grasp feasibility on thin curved dishware, (2) reachability and placement into constrained rack slots, and (3) protocols that map a manipulator's hardware limits per object type and target location? Sources: 28 opened and verified (three bibliography agents, one verification pass, one synthesis), 2015-2026 with two foundational earlier works; search 2026-10-23, single pass, not systematic. The review-form note was shown; the author continued in lit-review mode.

# Synthesis: two-finger loading of mouth-up dishware into dishwasher racks (UR5e + Robotiq 2F-85)

Scope: 28 keyed sources from the three bibliographies ([Makhal2018] appears in two lists; [suhkim2025] and the former [Kim2025] are the same Chalmers thesis). Own-setting facts come from `code/frigidaire/docs/robot.md` and are not literature.

## (a) Themes, contradictions, evidence quality

**Theme 1: a top-down rim pinch on upright cups and bowls is feasible with a parallel jaw, including the 2F-85.** The one source on our exact gripper reports 0 failures in 36 bowl removals and 2 failures in cup-only scenes over 45 physical trials, pinching a random rim point with the jaws perpendicular to the rim tangent [srinivas2023]. Otsuji and Dobashi [otsuji2024] rim-grasped eight wet bowls (128-184 mm, 0.09-0.52 kg) with a 38 mm-stroke industrial gripper and placed every size into all 12 slots of a 3x4 rack under 5 mm initial error, measuring >= 39 N needed for wet ceramic. In simulation, bowl-rim grasps reached 0.91 upright and mug rims 0.96 [simeonov2022]; with a custom pinch gripper, cups were removed 17 of 19 detected [voysey2021]. Four sources converge, two on real dishes; evidence is moderate because every N is <= 36 per class and only one source uses a 2F-85.

**Theme 2: the bowl result is conditional on an explicit rim-grasp family.** Generic learned samplers scored 0.10-0.15 on four bowls and 0.00 on the YCB bowl, because a bowl wider than the opening must be rim-grasped and neither method made that mode switch [alliegro2022]. This is a conditional difference, not a contradiction of Theme 1: the demonstration-anchored [simeonov2022] and hand-coded [srinivas2023] rim grasps succeed where the sampler fails. Quality: [alliegro2022] is hardware but admits a train/deploy gripper mismatch; [simeonov2022] is PyBullet with success defined as stable contact after closing, not a lift.

**Theme 3: a plate lying flat is the hard class for a rigid two-finger gripper.** Every source that tried it found the fingertips entering the support. A one-shot pinch removed 12 of 24 detected plates; a force-guided primitive (lower the jaws to the tray, rise 2 mm, slide 4 cm toward the plate centre, close on the edge) raised this to 23 of 24 [voysey2021]. Series-elastic fingers that comply against the table and slide under the lip pick dishes into a rack, qualitatively only [castroornelas2024]. A stock hobby gripper could not grasp a flat paper plate at all without propping; propped plates hung by the edge and dropped, 10/25 picked [suhkim2025]. The 23/24 versus 10/25 gap resolves on primitive and compliance (silicone pads plus a surface-contact slide versus a rigid jaw with neither). Quality is low on both sides: a custom gripper whose outcomes mix perception, grasp and placement, and toy hardware. No source uses a 2F-85 on a ceramic plate [MATERIAL GAP].

**Theme 4: compliance trades plate access for slip.** Compliant Fin-Ray jaws make slip the majority failure [jeon2026], a preprint with no per-object split; rigid Franka jaws slip in 5-7 % of trials [vanoort2024], a preprint with no per-category results; compliant fingers are what gets under a plate lip [castroornelas2024]. A conditional difference on low-weight sources. Other measured failures: a 4.5 cm jaw too short for stacks of 4+ and collision with a taller neighbour [srinivas2023]; closing pushes neighbouring cups [jeon2026]; straight jaws jammed on the small bowl and jaw-divider interference at release, which bent-jaw conditions were derived to clear [otsuji2024].

**Theme 5: reachability maps are kinematic and ignore the environment.** The line runs from directional voxel maps [Zacharias2007] through inverted maps for base placement [Vahrenkamp2013; Makhal2018] to a 4D map for arms with a free last wrist joint and a 360-degree base, which the UR5e satisfies, at 67x fewer cells and about 99 % true-positive rate [Rudorfer2025]; learned maps fall to 89.3 % IoU in SE(3) [Murooka2025]. All share one limit: no environment collisions. Collisions enter afterwards as IK and RRT-Connect filters in base-pose optimisation [Mayer2025], as 2D accessibility filters that reject about 62 % of candidates before IK on a stationary UR5e with a 2F-85 at a shelf [Wingender2026], or as precomputed swept-volume blocking labels on a roadmap [WangMiaoBekris2022].

**Theme 6: the insertion path, not the endpoint, decides execution.** Plans gated only on non-overlap executed 17.11 % of the time; plans gated on a compatible grasp plus IK and collision checks at every pose along a vertical insertion executed 79.1 % [WangHauser2019]. Geometrically valid shelf placements were sometimes kinematically infeasible, so several candidates are kept for IK and a planner to choose among [Simeonov2023], with no statistics on how often. A gated label per (grasp, placement), IK then RRT-Connect then PhysX, predicted IK at 0.982 and collision at 0.968, but the labels were physics-free while execution slipped [Liu2025]. Four sources converge; none has tines or an overhanging rack, and [Wingender2026] explicitly omits the board above.

**Theme 7: a fixed base fails at the reach boundary and next to the base, for kinematic or collision reasons, not grasp quality.** A 256-cell sweep at 4 cm pitch with 5 repetitions found >= 80 % centrally, near 0 beside the base and low at the boundary [Bartunek2026]; a fixed HSR base reached 40 % versus 81.7 % with planned placement, its far row 0/5 from table collisions [Saini2024]; allowing base yaw lifted edge tasks from about 15 % to about 55 % [Mayer2025]. Our own mount search (7/7 starts, 6/7 goals from one floor mount) fits this pattern.

**Theme 8: hardware limits are reported as staged gate tables with repeated trials per cell.** Reachability, graspability (payload, aperture), lift-and-hold success and wrist-rotation stability are separate scores, and an object enters the final score only if it passes the early gates [Bottarel2020]; objects are pre-filtered by payload and size [Mahler2017]; set points are fixed small offsets [Calli2015]. Grasp corpora label per grasp with no arm [Eppner2021; Fang2020] and say nothing about reach.

Two bibliography-internal inconsistencies were checked against the PDFs before compilation (see the verification section): [voysey2021] closed-loop plates (23/24 in the entry, 23/23 in the summary) and the Chalmers plate full-task rate (1/25 vs 12 %).

## (b) Established versus thin

Established (moderate strength): rim pinch on upright cups and bowls with a parallel jaw [srinivas2023; otsuji2024; voysey2021; simeonov2022]; the kinematic-map toolbox and its collision blindness [Zacharias2007; Makhal2018; Rudorfer2025]; path-gated placement beating endpoint-gated placement [WangHauser2019; Liu2025; Simeonov2023]; the shape of a fixed-base feasibility map [Bartunek2026; Saini2024]; the staged gate protocol [Bottarel2020].

Thin. Sample sizes: N <= 36 per dish class on hardware, n = 5 per cell in sweeps, 5 grasps per object in GRASPA, and the tableware sources mix perception, grasp and placement failures [voysey2021; jeon2026]. Simulation versus hardware: [simeonov2022], [Liu2025], [Eppner2021] and [Fang2020] are simulation only; [alliegro2022] trained on a 2F-85 and deployed on a Franka; [Bartunek2026] checked 83.1 % simulated against 85.7 % real on a single can. Gripper class: of nine sub-question-1 sources only [srinivas2023] runs a 2F-85, and it has no plates; the plate evidence comes from a custom pinch gripper [voysey2021], series-elastic fingers [castroornelas2024] and a hobby arm [suhkim2025]. Racks: only [otsuji2024] (bowls placed mouth-down, industrial gripper, known positions) and [voysey2021] (household rack, no per-slot data) load an actual rack, and no source reports per-slot rack feasibility in Isaac, MuJoCo or PyBullet.

## (c) Gaps that matter for our question

1. Plate pick with a rigid 2F-85 from a flat counter: no data [MATERIAL GAP]. No source measures a dish pivoting about the jaw axis on a flared rim, so our ~30 mm bowl pivot has no external comparator.
2. Per-slot reachability for a fixed UR5e into a rack with tines, the lower rack below the mount and the upper rack overhead: no source [MATERIAL GAP]. [Rudorfer2025] records only pz >= 0 (reader-inferred in the bibliography; verify in the paper), so RM4D cannot serve the lower rack as published.
3. IK-branch choice under an overhang: the only explicit criterion is the IK-solution count per pose, at most 8 for a 6-DoF arm [Makhal2018].
4. Mouth-down grasping: no source attempts a foot-ring or base grasp on an inverted plate or bowl; [otsuji2024] grasps mouth-up and only places mouth-down. The hypothesis that mouth-down dishes are ungraspable is untested, not established [MATERIAL GAP].
5. Grip force on wet dishes for the 2F-85: the 39 N threshold is for rubber-tipped PLA jaws on another gripper [otsuji2024].
6. Release interference between stock 2F-85 pads and tines for a mouth-up bowl: [otsuji2024] gives jaw-bend conditions for dividers and mouth-down bowls only.

## (d) Implications for the experiment design

**Grasp family per object.** Cups: top-down rim pinch is supported by [srinivas2023], [voysey2021] and [simeonov2022]; handle grasps rest on [vanoort2024] alone. Upright bowls: top-down pinch at a clear rim point with jaws perpendicular to the rim tangent is supported by [srinivas2023] (r 8.5 cm bowl, 0/36) and [otsuji2024]; a generic grasp sampler is not [alliegro2022]. Mouth-down bowl foot-ring clamp: unsupported by any source, consistent with our unreliable result. Plates: the literature does not support a one-shot rigid pinch on a plate lying flat (12/24 [voysey2021], 10/25 [suhkim2025]), so "everything mouth-up" does not make plates easy; the only numbered recipe is the surface-contact slide (lower, rise 2 mm, slide 4 cm, close on the edge) [voysey2021], and whether a stock 2F-85 pad gets under a HOTEC plate rim is [MATERIAL GAP].

**Reachability representation.** Do not build a voxel map: every map source is collision-blind [Makhal2018; Rudorfer2025], RM4D excludes pz < 0, and the quantity we need is per slot. Index the table by (object type, rack slot, approach yaw about world z); yaw is the one degree the UR5e wrist-3 freedom that RM4D exploits does not absorb once the jaw must clear tines. Per cell, enumerate all analytic IK branches (up to 8 [Makhal2018]) and record the fraction of yaws with a collision-free IK solution for arm plus held object at pre-grasp, grasp, lift, pre-place and release (a per-slot reachability index, D = R/N as in [Makhal2018]), then the fraction with an RRT-Connect path, the gate that moved execution from 17 % to 79 % in [WangHauser2019], with the upper rack and door in the FCL world because no source models the overhang [Wingender2026]. For a mount at z = 0.90 m the lower rack is a below-base query that the existing analytic IK plus FCL stack already answers; the map is the per-slot output of that stack, not a precomputed grid.

**Gate sequence and sample size.** Follow [Bottarel2020] and [Liu2025]: G0 slot pose reachable by IK on any branch; G1 collision-free IK at every waypoint; G2 RRT-Connect path within a fixed budget, with budget failures counted separately as [Liu2025] warns; G3 grasp holds through lift and transport in Isaac (0.15 m lift held 5 s, per [Bottarel2020]); G4 release settles in the slot and `evaluate_placement` passes. Report every gate per cell, and admit a cell to the summary only if G0-G1 pass. G0-G2 are deterministic up to the planner seed; the variance comes from initial-pose perturbation (5 mm in [otsuji2024], 1 cm offsets in [Calli2015]), the rim point [srinivas2023] and the seed. n = 5 per cell, as in [Bartunek2026] and [Saini2024], leaves a 5/5 cell with an exact (Clopper-Pearson) 95 % lower bound of 0.48; 10/10 gives 0.69 and 20/20 gives 0.83 (own arithmetic). Use n = 10 per (object, slot) with Clopper-Pearson intervals and raise n only on cells that are neither 10/10 nor 0/10. One object type per sweep is sound for G0-G2 and keeps the three types parallel, but the neighbour effects in [srinivas2023], [jeon2026] and [otsuji2024] make the single-object table an upper bound; a loaded-rack pass must follow.

**The plate case.** With a rigid two-finger gripper, success on a flat plate depends on a primitive that uses the support, not on reach, so a plate sweep run as a top-down pinch would measure the gripper, not the slots. Run the plate grasp gate on the counter first with n = 25 (the two plate datasets used 24-25 [voysey2021; suhkim2025]), comparing the slide primitive against a rim pinch on a pre-tilted plate; if neither reaches about 20/25, skip the plate reachability sweep and report the plate as a gripper limit.

## (e) Five limitations of this review

1. Gripper transfer: only [srinivas2023] uses a 2F-85; all plate evidence is from custom or toy grippers, so every plate conclusion is inferred across hardware.
2. Small N throughout (<= 36 per dish class, n = 5 per sweep cell), and tableware outcomes mix perception, grasp and placement with no per-object split in [jeon2026] and [vanoort2024].
3. Reachability evidence is kinematic, collision-blind and never on a rack; the RM4D pz >= 0 reading and the [Fang2020] weakness are reader-inferred; [Vahrenkamp2013]'s own numbers were not read and [Zacharias2007] was read through citing papers.
4. Six sources are preprints ([jeon2026], [vanoort2024], [Wingender2026], [Saini2024], [Liu2025], [Bartunek2026]), and relevant work was excluded unverified, partly behind paywalls: Jiang et al. 2012 (plates in a dish rack), Qi et al. 2025 (plate insertion), Kinugawa et al. 2022, and a cross-embodiment gripper benchmark with Wilson intervals.
5. Two internal number conflicts (resolved in the verification section), a single-pass non-systematic search on 2026-10-23, and own-setting results (3/3, 7/7, 6/7) that are tiny and unreviewed.

## What this literature says
1. A top-down rim pinch with a 2F-85-class jaw is feasible and fairly reliable on upright cups and bowls when a rim point is clear of neighbours and the wall fits the jaw depth: 0 failures in 36 bowl removals and 2 in 36 cup removals [srinivas2023]; cups 17 of 19 detected [voysey2021]; simulated bowl-rim grasps 0.91 [simeonov2022]; wet ceramic bowls held at >= 39 N [otsuji2024].
2. Generic learned planners fail on bowls wider than the opening because a rim grasp is required (0.10-0.15, YCB bowl 0.00) [alliegro2022]; rim-aware or demonstration-anchored grasps avoid this [simeonov2022; srinivas2023].
3. Plates are the hard class: a low rim on the support puts the fingertips into the support. What works: force-guided surface contact then a horizontal slide onto the edge (12/24 one-shot, 23/24 closed-loop) [voysey2021]; fingers that comply and slide under the lip [castroornelas2024]; a rigid hobby gripper could not lift a flat plate [suhkim2025].
4. Measured failure modes: slip (majority with compliant jaws [jeon2026], 5-7 % with rigid [vanoort2024]); weak edge grips that drop plates [voysey2021; suhkim2025]; grasp-depth errors [vanoort2024]; jaw height too short for stacks and collisions with taller neighbours [srinivas2023]; neighbour displacement on closing [jeon2026]; jaw-rack interference at release [otsuji2024].
5. Gap: no included source measures a plate pivoting about the jaw axis on a flared rim; none reports per-object success for a rigid 2F-85 on ceramic plates on a counter; dish numbers are small samples (N <= 36 per class) mixing perception and grasp failures.
6. Net: cup and bowl rim pinches can be treated as feasible given rim clearance of about one finger width and enough jaw depth [srinivas2023; otsuji2024]; a plate pick needs a support-contact slide-in primitive or a raised rim [voysey2021; castroornelas2024]; the plate evidence is thin and mostly qualitative.

## What this literature says
1. Reachability representations went from directional voxel maps [Zacharias2007] to inverted maps for base placement [Vahrenkamp2013; Makhal2018]; for UR5e-class arms a 4D map matches them with about 67x fewer cells [Rudorfer2025]; learned maps fall to 89 % IoU in SE(3) [Murooka2025].
2. Base-pose optimisation with collision-free IK and path filters solves over 90 % of real factory tasks but at most 57 % of hard ones; letting the base yaw lifts edge cases from about 15 % to about 55 % [Mayer2025]; the IK-solution count per pose is the only explicit IK-branch criterion found [Makhal2018].
3. The maps ignore environment collisions [Makhal2018; Rudorfer2025]; collisions enter afterwards as filters [Mayer2025; Wingender2026] or precomputed swept-volume blocking labels [WangMiaoBekris2022].
4. For constrained-slot placement, checking the insertion path decides execution: IK- and collision-checked top-down paths execute 79 % of the time vs 17 % for overlap-only plans [WangHauser2019]; learned shelf-slot placement reaches 0.85-0.94 in simulation but some geometrically valid slots are kinematically infeasible [Simeonov2023].
5. Confined side-access shelves: precomputed blocking labels [WangMiaoBekris2022] and cheap accessibility filters removing about 62 % of candidates before IK [Wingender2026]; none models an overhanging board above the slot.
6. Gap: no verified source measures how reachable dishwasher rack slots are for a fixed UR5e (tines, a lower rack below the mount, the upper rack overhead) or how the IK-branch choice affects insertion under an overhang.

## What this literature says
1. Per-object hardware limits are reported as a staged gate table, not one rate: reachability, graspability (payload/aperture), success kept separate [Bottarel2020]; objects filtered by payload and size before trials [Mahler2017]; fixed small offset set points [Calli2015].
2. Per-location feasibility comes as kinematic maps (reachability index per voxel [Makhal2018], 6 board regions [Bottarel2020]) or executed sweeps with n repetitions per grid cell (256 x 5 in PyBullet [Bartunek2026], 12 x 5 on a real HSR [Saini2024]).
3. Every grid sweep found the same structure: success falls to near 0 at the reach boundary and next to the base, for kinematic or collision reasons, not grasp quality [Bartunek2026; Saini2024].
4. Simulation feasibility pipelines gate in sequence: IK, planner, physics execution [Liu2025]; grasp corpora label per grasp without the arm [Eppner2021; Fang2020].
5. Dish and tableware evidence is thin and small-n [suhkim2025; Qi 2025]; no study found reports per-slot rack feasibility in Isaac, MuJoCo or PyBullet.
6. The shape that fits one arm x one object type x all rack positions: the per-cell repeated-trial grid [Bartunek2026; Saini2024] with cells indexed by rack slot, each cell carrying a gate breakdown (reachable -> IK and collision-free path -> grasp holds -> settles in slot) [Bottarel2020; Liu2025], and n with a binomial interval, since n = 5 leaves per-cell rates coarse.
