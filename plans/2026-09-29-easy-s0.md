## 4. Hypotheses to test first
These come from a review of the previous report. Confirm or refute each one with evidence.

**H1: the arm "lag" is mostly gravity sag.**
- Fix 4 handled the velocity-dependent part, yet R7 still needs 0.06–0.08 rad limits.
- With PD position drives, steady-state error is roughly gravity torque divided by stiffness, so it grows with reach and payload.
- On a shoulder or elbow joint, 0.06–0.08 rad is roughly 3–7 cm at the fingertips (lever 0.45–0.9 m). That is several times the 10–15 mm per-side clearance of a rim pinch.

**H2: the conversion dropped the 2F-85's linkage coupling (mimic joints or loop closures).**
- Fix 3 is a symptom of this.
- With independently driven inner joints, the fingertips may rotate under load. A rim pinch's gravity load acts about the rim tangent, which is parallel to the finger joint axes, so the load drives exactly this rotation. That would explain the ~30 mm pivot.
- A real 2F-85 keeps its fingertips parallel through the linkage, and real rim pinches do hold (the Busboy paper, arXiv 2307.03882, grasped bowls by the rim with a UR5 + 2F-85).

**H3: the pad material had no effect for a mechanical reason.** Candidates:
- PhysX applies the higher-priority friction combine mode of the pair (average < min < multiply < max). A bowl material set to "min" cancels any pad friction increase.
- The material may be bound to a prim that isn't the live collider after un-instancing.
- Slip may not be friction-limited at all (see H2 and H4).

**H4: the bowl's physics model is wrong.**
- Its mass may be computed from a filled collision volume, making it too heavy.
- Its collider may lose the cavity, the rim or the foot-ring recess.

**H5: the pedestal verdict assumed the old placement.**
- "No pedestal pose reaches all goals" was computed for top-down, mouth-up placement.
- Holding the bowl by its robot-side rim puts the bowl body 12–18 cm beyond the fingertips. That is about the depth of the under-counter strip.

**H6: some goals may be inaccessible to the bowl alone.**
- With the upper rack pushed in, some lower-rack goals may have no collision-free path even for the bowl by itself.
- The teleport benchmark only checked that goals are stable, not that they are reachable.

### Phase 2: grasp library

**Design principle: bring every bowl into one in-hand state.** That state is a rim pinch with the bowl hanging below the fingers, its center of mass directly under the grasp. For a thin hemispherical bowl the mouth then faces sideways and about 27° up; the angle depends on how deep the center of mass sits.
- The ~30 mm pivot is the bowl moving toward this equilibrium.
- If the carry starts there, gravity has nothing left to pivot. The in-hand pose becomes predictable, on the real robot too.
- Record the settled in-hand transform. Use it for placement and in the planner's carried-bowl collision model. With an accurate model, fix 12's rise to 1.25 m may no longer be needed.

**Rim pinch, all variants:**
- **Jaw axis:** along the wall normal at the chosen rim point, perpendicular to the rim tangent (the Busboy grasp rule).
- **Approach:** along the wall slope, from the rim edge.
- **Depth:** 15–25 mm below the rim, with fingertips clear of the support surface.
- **Pre-grasp:** 80–100 mm back along the approach.
- **Opening:** wall thickness plus about 10–15 mm per side, no wider. The inner finger must fit inside the curve.
- **Close target:** fully closed, so an early stop means "object detected". The jaw-angle filter stands in for the real gripper's object detection.
- **Speed:** about 45–70 mm/s.
- **Force:** about 105–150 N (40–60% of the 20–235 N scale). The Reference numbers section explains why.

**G1: upright bowl.**
- Use the existing top pinch, testing several rim angles θ. Phase 3 chooses θ for each goal.
- Compare two lifts:
  - **A:** lift straight up.
  - **B:** keep the far rim on the counter as a pivot. Rotate the bowl about it until the center of mass is directly under the grasp (about 63° for a hemispherical bowl), then lift. The counter carries the moment during the rotation.

**G2: lying bowl.**
- Settle the scene first and plan from the settled pose.
- If the bowl rolls without robot contact, report it as a scene issue.
- Pinch the rim at its highest point, approaching from the mouth side along the wall slope, with the jaw axis along the wall normal (roughly vertical).
- Center the open jaws on the wall so both fingers touch at nearly the same time. The jaw forces are then vertical and pass near the counter contact, so they shouldn't roll the bowl.
- This grasp is already close to the hanging state.

**G3: upside-down bowl. Slide it to the edge, then pinch.**
- **Push:** use the side of the closed fingers, low on the wall, with fingertips at least 3 mm above the counter. Push through the bowl's center toward the nearest free counter edge at 20–50 mm/s.
- **Stop point:** when the rim overhangs 25–40 mm. Keep the overhang ≤ rim radius − 25 mm, so the center of mass stays behind the edge.
- **Pinch:** inner finger under the wall inside the cavity, outer finger on the outer surface. Approach from in front of and below the edge, along the wall slope.
- **Finish:** pivot the bowl on its counter-supported far rim until the center of mass is under the grasp, then lift (as in G1-B). Don't let it swing into the hanging state after lift-off.
- **Paths:** plan push paths and bowl order so no neighbor is disturbed.
- **Precedent:** the slide-to-edge pre-grasp used for thin objects (Hang, Morgan & Dollar, RA-L 2019).

**G4: experimental, only for bowls G3 can't handle.**
- Tilt the bowl up against an existing fixed feature such as a backsplash or rack edge (Zhou & Held, "Learning to Grasp the Ungraspable with Emergent Extrinsic Dexterity", CoRL 2022).
- Or grasp outward inside the foot ring. The grasp is centered on the bowl's axis, and the bowl stays mouth-down, which is already the goal orientation, so no flip is needed. Even tilted 30° at the goal, the moment is only about 0.07 N·m. Only try this if the collider really has the recess and the outer faces of the fingers can press on it.
- The fallback is a regrasp area on the counter, as the PoliMi team used in the first WARA Robotics mobile manipulation challenge (arXiv 2505.06919). Any new fixture needs my approval.

**Accept a variant when:**
- acquisition succeeds at least 9/10 times on perturbed poses
- settle displacement is repeatable (σ ≤ 5 mm)
- drift after settling stays ≤ 5 mm and ≤ 3° through the carry and reorientation
- no neighbor is disturbed

This tightens R1 ("stable after it settles", which I accepted) by making the settle measured and repeatable.

### Phase 3: placement at the real goals

**Idea:** check whether the teleport benchmark shows each goal is stable when a bowl is teleported there. If so, the goals are resting poses. Then release the bowl inside each goal's basin of attraction instead of holding it exactly at the goal.

1. **Goal geometry.** For each goal, compute:
   - the axis tilt from straight down
   - the height of the highest rim point relative to the tine tops
   - clearances to neighbors, the counter and the upper rack

   Goal error should ignore rotation about the bowl's own axis. Keep the benchmark's definition if it already does; otherwise propose the change.
2. **Basin search (object only; label it as analysis).**
   - Sample release poses around each goal: raised 5–30 mm along the insertion direction, rotated 0–45° toward the hanging orientation, offset ±10 mm.
   - Teleport the bowl there at rest, release, settle, and score with the benchmark rule. Include the bowls placed earlier in the sequence.
   - Keep release poses whose ±5 mm / ±5° neighbors also succeed.
3. **Robot feasibility.**
   - Using the measured in-hand transform, check IK and collisions for pre-release (100 mm back along the insertion axis), release and retreat.
   - The last 100 mm must be a straight line. Avoid singularities by choosing grasps and IK branches with enough manipulability, not by swapping in joint moves.
   - Choose θ so the fingers hold the robot-side rim at release, with the bowl body extending away from the robot.
4. **Execution.**
   - Insert at ≤ 20 mm/s over the last 50 mm, with contact monitoring.
   - Stop on any arm or palm contact, or on bowl–rack contact above 5–10 N.
   - Open about 15 mm at ≤ 30% speed, then retreat along the insertion axis.

## Reference numbers
- **2F-85:** stroke 0–85 mm; force 20–235 N (±15%); speed 20–150 mm/s; mass about 0.9 kg.
- **UR5e:** 5 kg payload; 850 mm reach; ±360° joints except the elbow (±π in the UR description).
- **Rim pinch:** a 0.4 kg bowl held 75 mm from its center puts about 0.29 N·m on the grasp, about the rim tangent.
  - That axis lies in the pad plane, parallel to the finger joints, so torsional friction doesn't resist it.
  - Two things do: opposite friction at the inner and outer contacts, with the wall thickness as lever, and pad pressure shifting along the wall, which needs fingertips that don't rotate.
  - With μ = 0.5 and a 5 mm wall, holding takes about 120 N with point-like contacts, and about 35–50 N with 2–3 mm of contact half-height along the wall. Use 105–150 N.
- **Hanging state:** a thin hemispherical bowl's center of mass sits about R/2 behind the rim plane, so a rim-pinched bowl hangs with its mouth about 27° above horizontal.
- **Dishwasher convention:** bowls face down, angled toward the center and the spray, between tines (Whirlpool). Mouth-up bowls collect water, which is why R5 and R6 can't be part of the headline.
